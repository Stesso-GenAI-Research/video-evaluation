# Writing in this repository

The author's October 15 writing samples develop an idea through a concrete
problem, the work undertaken to address it, and what the outcome means. Apply
that progression to the research paper and documentation. The examples establish
a voice; their personal experiences and unrelated statistics do not belong in
this project.

Use first person when describing the author's research choices. Explain why a
method was tried before introducing its details, and connect results to the
next question they raise. Prefer complete paragraphs with natural transitions
over a succession of short declarations or numbered claims. Keep the language
accessible to an undergraduate reader while retaining the equations and
definitions needed to reproduce the experiment.

Use conventional paper sections, simple tables, and unembellished typography.
Avoid decorative callouts, repeated status labels, a heading for every short
paragraph, and instructions to the writer inside the main argument. Unfinished
sections should have short, specific placeholders. CLI documentation can use
headings, lists, and code blocks where they help someone carry out a task.

Describe what the evidence supports. Human-written source annotations are not
the same as human-observed preferences. A successful program run is not an
accuracy result, and a selected diagnostic sample is not an independent test.
Explain those distinctions where they affect interpretation without repeating
the same qualification in every paragraph. Do not invent personal experiences,
reviewer judgments, study decisions, or positive findings to match the voice.
